# Research a decision

Define the decision the research must support and the facts needed to make it. Inspect supplied artifacts and local instructions first when appropriate; consult current documentation for changing APIs, product features, installation formats, or tool availability.

Prefer original documentation and primary sources. Fetch the relevant source rather than relying on search snippets. Distinguish documented behavior, local observations, and inference. Record source URLs and the research date when the report will be reused.

Evaluate candidate skills by actual task coverage, clear invocation boundaries, resource requirements, and maintenance burden. Inspect their instructions before recommending or copying them. Avoid wholesale third-party prompt collections and do not copy licensed material without checking its terms. Prefer a short original skill when the requirement is specific.

Return the recommendation, supporting evidence, alternatives that materially affect the decision, missing capabilities, and remaining uncertainty. Stop when the decision is supported; research breadth is not a deliverable unless requested. Research does not authorize installing software, connecting accounts, or publishing anything.
